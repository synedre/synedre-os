# Synedre Trademark Policy

**Version 0.1 — draft.** Last updated: 2026-08-18.

## 1. In short

**The code is free. The name is not.**

Our software is released under the GNU Affero General Public License v3.0.
That licence gives you extensive freedoms over the *code*: to use it, study
it, modify it, and redistribute it, commercially or not. We want you to
exercise those freedoms fully.

Trademarks answer a different question. They exist so that when someone sees
the name **Synedre** or **CodeMyShop** on a piece of software, a service, or
a website, they can tell where it comes from and who stands behind it. If
anyone could put our name on anything, that signal would be worthless — to
users first, and to us second.

This policy explains where the line sits. It is deliberately permissive: most
of what you are likely to want to do needs no permission at all.

## 3. Relationship to the software licence

Nothing in this policy restricts any right the AGPL-3.0 grants you over the
code. You may fork, modify, and redistribute exactly as the licence allows.

Section 7(e) of the AGPL-3.0 expressly permits terms "declining to grant
rights under trademark law for use of some trade names, trademarks, or
service marks". This policy is such a term. It is not an additional
restriction on the software, and it does not make any copy of the software
non-free.

Put plainly: **the licence gives you the code, not the name.** If you exercise
your licence rights in a way that requires you to stop using our name, you
have lost nothing the licence promised you.

## 4. What you may do without asking us

You do not need our permission to:

- **Say what the software is.** "Built on CodeMyShop", "compatible with
  CodeMyShop", "CodeMyShop hosting", "we migrate shops to CodeMyShop" — as
  long as the statement is true and it is clear you are describing a
  relationship to our software, not claiming to be us.
- **Talk about the project.** Articles, blog posts, tutorials, videos, talks,
  training material, courses, books, reviews, criticism. Including critical
  ones. You may use our name in the title.
- **Redistribute unmodified copies** under their original name — packages,
  mirrors, containers, distribution builds that ship the software as we
  released it.
- **Use our logos to illustrate a discussion of the project**, at their
  correct proportions and without alteration.
- **Refer to a fork by its relationship to us**: "a fork of CodeMyShop",
  "based on CodeMyShop". What you may not do is *name* it with our mark — see
  section 6.
- **Use the name in a repository, issue, or commit** where you are working on
  or with the project.

Academic, journalistic, and comparative uses are covered by law in most
jurisdictions regardless of this policy. We do not intend to interfere with
them.

## 5. What requires our written permission

Please ask us before you:

- Use one of our marks, or a name confusingly close to one, as the **name of
  your product, service, company, application, or distribution**.
- Distribute a **modified version** under our name (see section 6).
- Register a **domain name**, social media account, package name, or
  organisation account containing one of our marks.
- Use our marks on **merchandise** (clothing, stickers, physical goods).
- Present yourself as **certified, official, partnered with, endorsed by, or
  affiliated with** the project or its maintainers, or use our marks in a way
  that would suggest it.
- Use our **logos as your own** logo, or as an element of one.

Permission is often granted, and it is free. We mostly want to know.

## 6. Modified versions and forks

This is the rule that matters most to us.

If you distribute a **modified** version of the software, you must either
obtain our permission or **remove our marks and logos** from it — from the
product name, the user interface, the documentation, and the package name.

You remain free to state accurately what your work derives from: *"NewName,
a fork of CodeMyShop"* is fine and always will be. What is not fine is
shipping *"CodeMyShop"* with changes we did not make, because anyone
encountering the result will attribute those changes to us.

The same applies to builds: if you patch, repackage, or re-bundle the
software in a way that changes its behaviour, the name has to move with the
responsibility.

## 7. Package names and namespaces

Official packages are published under our own namespaces (for example the
`@codemyshop/` npm scope). Do not publish packages under our marks or in a
namespace that suggests they are ours. Prefix your own instead
(`yourname-codemyshop-adapter` rather than `codemyshop-adapter`), or ask us.

## 8. Using the marks correctly

When you do use our marks, please:

- **Keep the spelling and capitalisation**: *Synedre*, *CodeMyShop*. Not
  SYNEDRE, not Code My Shop, not codemyshop as a product name in running
  text.
- **Use them as adjectives, not nouns or verbs**: "a CodeMyShop store", not
  "a CodeMyShop" or "to CodeMyShop a site".
- **Do not alter the logos** — no recolouring, stretching, cropping, adding
  elements, or combining them with other marks.
- **Do not create derivative marks**: names like *CodeMyShop Pro*,
  *SynedreCloud*, or *Synedre-anything* are exactly what section 5 covers.

## 9. Asking for permission

Write to **trademark@synedre.com**, telling us who you are, what you want to
use, and how. We aim to answer within two weeks. Describing the intended use
concretely — a screenshot, a URL, a mock-up — gets you a faster and usually
more generous answer than an abstract request.

If you think a use of yours is already covered by section 4 but you would
rather be certain, ask anyway. We would much rather answer a question than
send a letter.

## 10. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy. Changes apply from the date they are published and
are not retroactive: a permission we granted stays granted under the terms it
was granted on.
